16. Explain the basic concepts involved in plant tissue culture. 
Basic concepts of plant tissue culture are

a) Totipotency

b) Differentiation

c) dedifferentiation
d) redifferentiation

a) Totipotency

      The property of live plant cells that they have the genetic

potential when cultured in nutrient medium to give rise to a
complete individual plant.

b) Differentiation

      i) The process of biochemical and structural changes by which

cells become specialized in form and function.

     ii) Meristematic tissue into mature cells.
c) Dedifferentiation

     i) The reversion of mature cells to the meristematic state leading

to the formation of callus is called dedifferentiation.

    ii) Mature cells into the meristematic tissue.
    iii) Formation of callus is called dedifferentiation.

d) Redifferentiation

    The component cells of callus have the ability to form a whole

plant in a nutrient medium is called redifferentiation

Explain the  major reproductive events in human beings ?

i) Gametogenesis: Formation of gametes by spermatogenesis and
oogenesis.

ii) Insemination: Transfer of sperms by the male into the female

genital tract.

iii) Fertilization: Fusion of male and female gametes to form zygote,

called Fertilization.
 iv) Cleavage: Rapid mitotic divisions of the zygote which convert the

single celled zygote into a multicellular structure called blastocyst.

v) Implantation: Attachment of blastocyst to the uterine wall.

vi) Placentation: Formation of placenta which is the intimate
connection between foetus and uterine wall of the mother for
exchange of nutrients.

vii) Gastrulation: Process by which blastocyst is changed into a

gastrula with three primary germ layers.
viii) Organogenesis: Formation of specific tissues, organs and organ

systems from three germ layers.

ix)  Parturition: Expulsion of the foetus from the mother’s womb

Why do we find a decrease in biodiversity distribution, if we move
from the tropics towards the poles?

i) The most important pattern of biodiversity is latitudinal gradient

in diversity.

ii) This means that there is an increasing diversity from the poles to

equator.
iii) Diversity increases as one moves towards the temperate zones

and reaches the maximum at the tropics.

iv) Thus, tropics harbour more biodiversity than temperate or polar
regions, especially between the latitudes of 23.5⁰N and 23.5⁰S

(Tropic of Cancer to the Tropic of Capricorn).

v) Harsh conditions exist in temperate areas during the cold seasons

while very harsh conditions prevail for most of the year in polar
regions.
